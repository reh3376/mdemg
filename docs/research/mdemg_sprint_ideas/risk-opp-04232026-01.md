## 2. Executive Summary

MDEMG has completed 7 development phases, 611 Go files, 150 API endpoints, 2,381 tests, and shipped a fine-tuned Qwen3-14B model (Phase 5 SFT). The system is architecturally strong in its core memory, retrieval, Jiminy, RSIC, and J17 layers. However, a structured analysis identifies **nine risks** and **nine opportunities** that must be addressed to bridge the gap between the current implementation and the VISION.md end-state.

The most consequential themes are:

- **Community & contribution structure** — the project has zero external contributors; the vision requires community collaboration.
- **Model & deployment portability** — the FT-LoRA track is implicitly Mac/MLX-first, while the vision targets cross-platform Docker-first deployment.
- **Scale ceilings** — DBSCAN O(n²) and two parallel decay systems threaten the emergence mechanic at the precise scale the system targets.
- **North-star FT objective** — FT-OAI-003 is blocked on authorization; the "cheap FT ≈ prod" thesis is unverified.
- **Multi-agent completeness** — Phase 36 (Observation Forwarding) remains unimplemented, leaving the multi-agent story operationally incomplete.

Opportunities cluster around: completing Phase 10 to unlock Sprint F (GRPO/DPO), externalizing J17 as a standalone protocol, publishing research (Concept Merging, GAT, Edge Type Attention), MCP marketplace presence, and industrial SME ingestion modules.

  - At least one non-Apple inference path documented and smoke-tested (e.g., llama.cpp GGUF export, vLLM, or ONNX Runtime).
  - Training reproducibility doc with hardware alternatives (e.g., A100/H100 approximate timing).
  - CI job verifying manifest SHAs against the configured store.
- **Suggested Epics / Stories:**
  - Epic 1: Artifact storage + retrieval CLI.
  - Epic 2: Cross-platform inference matrix (MLX / llama.cpp / vLLM) with a compatibility table.
  - Epic 3: Reproducibility & disaster-recovery runbook.
- **Dependencies:** Partially gates R4, O7, O8.
- **Effort Estimate:** Medium-Large (1–2 sprints).
- **Success Metrics:** model_pull_e2e_time < 10 min on broadband; cross_platform_inference_parity ≥ 95% of benchmark set.

### R3 — DBSCAN Performance Cliff at Scale

- **Severity:** High
- **Category:** Scalability / Core Algorithm
- **Current State:** DBSCAN clustering is O(n²) on CPU; 10–15 minutes for 8,000+ nodes; ~50K nodes is the practical prohibitive threshold. GPU acceleration flagged "research, low priority" in AGENT_HANDOFF.md.
- **Target State:** Emergence mechanic scales linearly or near-linearly to ≥500K nodes with bounded wall-clock time, without regressing clustering quality beyond an agreed tolerance.
- **Acceptance Criteria:**
  - Benchmarking harness for clustering that tracks: wall-clock, peak memory, cluster purity, and emergence quality at 10K / 50K / 250K / 500K node graphs.
  - At least one approximate alternative implemented behind a feature flag (candidates: HDBSCAN with index acceleration, LSH-based clustering, mini-batch k-means, or graph-native community detection like Leiden).
  - Regression gate: emergence quality regression ≤ 5% vs. DBSCAN on 8K baseline.
  - Performance target: 50K nodes clustered in ≤ 60s on reference hardware.
- **Suggested Epics / Stories:** (See also O6 — this risk and O6 are paired.)
  - Epic 1: Scalable clustering benchmarking harness.
  - Epic 2: Alternative clustering implementations behind feature flag.
  - Epic 3: Emergence quality regression gate in CI.
- **Dependencies:** Paired with O6; unlocks enterprise-scale market claims (O2, O5).
- **Effort Estimate:** Large (2–3 sprints; research + impl + regression).
- **Success Metrics:** clustering_p95_50k ≤ 60s; emergence_quality_delta_vs_dbscan ≤ 5%.
